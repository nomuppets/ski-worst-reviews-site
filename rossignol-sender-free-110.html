Rossignol Sender Free 110

Manufacturing / durability Conditions / performance Fit / stance / demand

The Sender Free 110 has the nastiest smoke in the set. The complaints are not just about style; durability and mount-point issues show up loudly enough to matter.

Manufacturing / durability

Durability smoke

The agent found credible complaints about delamination, snapped skis, and sidewall impact delams. The signal was not subtle. This is the main red flag.

Conditions / performance

Forward mount can punish directional skiers

Several reviewers and owners disliked the amount of tip in deep snow. Directional skiers reported tip-dive or over-the-bars feelings and often preferred mounting back.

Fit / stance / demand

Heavy, stiff, and tiring

The ski rewards aggression, but the stiff tail and mass can punish backseat skiing. In trees, bumps, and moguls it can become work.

Conditions / performance

Firm snow caveats

At 110 mm with long tails, it is not ideal when things get very firm. It wants soft snow, space, and a skier who stays on top of it.

Noise, not conviction

Factory tune and base-flatness complaints appeared, but not strongly enough to call a broad defect. Sizing awkwardness is more preference than failure.

Hokkaido verdict

Medium-high risk. Take it if you ski aggressively, like a centered freeride stance, and inspect gear carefully. Avoid it as your only Hokkaido ski if you are a traditional tip-driver or want bombproof durability.
