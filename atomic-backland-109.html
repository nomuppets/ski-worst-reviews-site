Atomic Backland 109

Manufacturing / durability Conditions / performance Fit / stance / demand

The Backland 109 is a soft-snow touring ski. The complaints are predictable and important: too light for ugly resort snow, too wide for some firm touring days, and not built to be a charger.

Manufacturing / durability

Too light in bad snow

Reviewers and users complain about tip deflection, squirrely behavior, and speed limits in crud, chop, breakable crust, and tracked snow.

Manufacturing / durability

Firm snow limitations

Hardpack and icy exits bring chatter and less reliability. A wide touring ski is not the tool for edgehold heroics.

Conditions / performance

Too wide for some tours

For volcanoes, long days, firm sidehilling, or mixed spring objectives, users worry that 109 mm is overkill and annoying.

Conditions / performance

Needs the right skier

It suits experienced skiers with a neutral stance better than hard-driving tip crushers or novices.

Noise, not conviction

One ugly edge blowout and denied-warranty complaint appeared twice, but the agent found it as a duplicated single-owner report rather than a systemic pattern.

Hokkaido verdict

Good for Hokkaido soft-snow touring. Do not buy it as a damp inbounds charger or a mixed-condition travel quiver. It wants powder, trees, and moderate speeds.
